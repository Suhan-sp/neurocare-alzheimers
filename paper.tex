\begin{abstract}
Early detection of Alzheimer's Disease (AD) is critical for timely therapeutic interventions and slowing cognitive decay. However, existing diagnostic paradigms rely heavily on expensive, invasive neuroimaging (PET/MRI) or spinal taps, while single-modality automated screening methods suffer from limited diagnostic robustness across diverse clinical presentations. This paper presents NeuroCare, a non-invasive, multimodal diagnostic decision-support system that synthesizes three distinct biomedical channels: neuropsychological cognitive metrics, 19-channel continuous electroencephalogram (EEG) signals, and acoustic speech biomarkers, enhanced by a computer-vision EEG report digitization engine. Cognitive features are processed from the OASIS longitudinal dataset ($N = 373$), yielding a baseline Regularized Logistic Regression model with 94.64\% validation accuracy (ROC-AUC: 0.9677). 19-channel continuous EEG signals ($N = 88$ human subjects, 1,320 clinical epochs) undergo Butterworth bandpass filtering (0.5--45 Hz), Welch Power Spectral Density (PSD) decomposition ($\delta, \theta, \alpha, \beta, \gamma$), Hjorth complexity analysis, and spectral slowing ratio ($\theta/\alpha$) computation, achieving 77.50\% accuracy (ROC-AUC: 0.8203) using an optimized XGBoost classifier. For clinics lacking digital signal files, a vision skeletonization digitizer parses scanned paper/PDF EEG reports with 95.0\% extraction confidence. Acoustic speech features (80+ parameters including 20 MFCCs, pitch $F_0$, micro-tremor Jitter/Shimmer, and pause ratios) derived from 591 clinical recordings achieve 93.02\% accuracy (ROC-AUC: 0.9806) with a Random Forest model. A constrained Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) ensemble layer fuses cross-modality probability distributions ($W_{\text{cognitive}} = 0.34, W_{\text{eeg}} = 0.33, W_{\text{speech}} = 0.33$), complemented by SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) for patient-level clinical interpretability. Experimental validation demonstrates that the multimodal ensemble achieves 96.85\% accuracy and an ROC-AUC of 0.9882, surpassing individual single-modality baselines and establishing a scalable, web-deployable framework for non-invasive early AD screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{lzheimer's} Disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline, memory impairment, speech disfluency, and altered cortical electrical activity. With global prevalence projected to triple by 2050, early diagnosis during preclinical or Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) stages is paramount for non-pharmacological interventions and clinical management.

Traditional diagnostic pipelines rely on Positron Emission Tomography (PET), structural Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers. Although accurate, these approaches are invasive, cost-prohibitive, and geographically constrained to specialized tertiary medical centers. Conversely, routine cognitive tests (e.g., Mini-Mental State Examination, MMSE) provide accessible screening but suffer from subjective scoring variability and practice effects.

Recent advancements in computer-aided diagnosis have investigated automated single-modality predictors, such as deep learning on structural MRI, spectral analysis of resting-state EEG, or voice acoustic feature modeling. However, single-modality systems face fundamental limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Cognitive scores alone cannot distinguish early pathophysiological neural changes prior to overt behavioral symptoms.
    \item EEG signals are susceptible to muscle/blink artifacts and environmental noise, limiting standalone sensitivity.
    \item Speech biomarkers vary across language, dialect, and emotional states, introducing individual variability.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these single-modality vulnerabilities, this work presents \textbf{NeuroCare}, an end-to-end multimodal predictive platform that integrates cognitive profiling, 19-channel EEG signal analysis, and speech acoustic processing, complemented by a computer-vision paper report digitization module. By combining non-invasive biomarkers spanning psychological, neurophysiological, and vocal domains into a unified ensemble framework with SHAP interpretability, NeuroCare achieves robust, accessible, and explainable diagnostic assessments suitable for clinical screening environments.

\section{Literature Survey}
The research landscape in automated Alzheimer's Disease diagnosis has evolved significantly across cognitive scoring, signal processing, acoustic feature extraction, and machine learning fusion techniques.

\subsection{Cognitive \& Neuropsychological Assessment}
Early computational models focused on longitudinal clinical records. Marcus \textit{et al.} introduced the Open Access Series of Imaging Studies (OASIS), establishing standardized benchmarks for Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR), and normalized Whole Brain Volume (nWBV) metrics \cite{marcus2007}. Machine learning classifiers on OASIS data demonstrated high accuracy for advanced stages \cite{tadepalli2021}; however, reliance on static cognitive scores fails to capture real-time functional neurophysiological degeneration.

\subsection{EEG Signal Processing \& Spectral Complexity}
Electroencephalography (EEG) provides high temporal resolution insight into cortical dynamics. Jeong \textit{et al.} demonstrated that AD progression is characterized by ``spectral slowing''---a shift in power distribution from high-frequency alpha ($\alpha$) and beta ($\beta$) bands toward lower-frequency delta ($\delta$) and theta ($\theta$) bands---and reduced non-linear signal complexity \cite{jeong2004}. Recent studies utilizing OpenNeuro benchmark datasets (ds004504) employ Butterworth filtering, Welch Power Spectral Density (PSD) estimation, and Hjorth mobility/complexity parameters \cite{babiloni2014}, \cite{gu2021}. Advanced classifiers such as XGBoost and 1D-CNNs have been applied to multi-channel EEG epochs to capture subtle neurodynamic disruptions.

\subsection{Acoustic Voice Biomarkers}
Vocal degradation serves as an early indicator of cognitive impairment. Research by Cummins \textit{et al.} identified key speech acoustic biomarkers associated with neurodegeneration, including Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), fundamental frequency ($F_0$) perturbations, Jitter (pitch instability), Shimmer (amplitude perturbation), and extended pause durations during cognitive tasks such as the ``Cookie Theft'' picture description test \cite{cummins2020}. Random Forest and SVM classifiers trained on acoustic feature sets achieve high sensitivity for mild dementia screening \cite{grisales2021}.

\subsection{Multimodal Information Fusion \& Explainability}
While single-modality systems show promise, multimodal data fusion offers significantly higher diagnostic resilience. Modern multimodal architectures typically utilize early feature concatenation, late decision-level soft voting, or stacking meta-classifiers \cite{achmad2023}. Moreover, the integration of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) frameworks, specifically SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), has become essential for clinical adoption, enabling physicians to trace automated risk scores back to underlying patient-specific biomarkers \cite{lundberg2017}, \cite{liu2023}.

\section{Methodology}
The proposed \textbf{NeuroCare} architecture operates through a multi-stage modular pipeline incorporating data acquisition, specialized signal/acoustic preprocessing, feature engineering, independent model optimization, constrained multimodal ensemble fusion, and XAI report generation.

\subsection{Modality 1: Cognitive Data Pipeline}
The cognitive evaluation layer processes $N = 373$ clinical patient records from the OASIS longitudinal dataset.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Features}: Age, Gender, Education (EDUC), Socioeconomic Status (SES), Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR), Estimated Total Intracranial Volume (eTIV), Normalized Whole Brain Volume (nWBV), and Atlas Scaling Factor (ASF).
    \item \textbf{Preprocessing}: Median imputation for missing SES values, standard z-score normalization:
    \begin{equation}
    x' = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Model}: Regularized Logistic Regression optimizing log-loss with $L_2$ penalty:
    \begin{equation}
    P(\text{AD}_{\text{cog}} | \mathbf{x}_{\text{cog}}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}' + b)}}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Modality 2: 19-Channel Clinical EEG Signal Pipeline}
The EEG processing engine handles 19-channel scalp recordings ($N = 88$ human subjects: 36 AD, 23 FTD, 29 CN) segmented into 1,320 clinical epochs.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Filtering}: A 4th-order zero-phase digital Butterworth bandpass filter ($0.5\text{ Hz} \le f \le 45\text{ Hz}$) removes DC offsets and muscle noise:
    \begin{equation}
    |H(f)|^2 = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{f}{f_c}\right)^{2N}}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Spectral Power Decomposition}: Welch's modified periodogram computes Power Spectral Density (PSD) across five canonical frequency bands: $\delta$ (0.5--4 Hz), $\theta$ (4--8 Hz), $\alpha$ (8--12 Hz), $\beta$ (12--30 Hz), and $\gamma$ (30--45 Hz).
    \item \textbf{Spectral Slowing Ratio}: Quantifies slow-wave synchronization:
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\theta/\alpha} = \frac{P_\theta}{P_\alpha}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Hjorth Time-Domain Parameters}: Calculates Activity, Mobility, and Complexity:
    \begin{align}
    \text{Activity} &= \text{Var}(y(t)) \\
    \text{Mobility} &= \sqrt{\frac{\text{Var}(y'(t))}{\text{Var}(y(t))}} \\
    \text{Complexity} &= \frac{\text{Mobility}(y'(t))}{\text{Mobility}(y(t))}
    \end{align}
    \item \textbf{Classification}: Processed using an optimized XGBoost gradient boosting classifier.
\end{enumerate}

Extracted continuous signal DataFrames are fed directly into the 19-channel `EEGPreprocessor` (Butterworth filter $\rightarrow$ Welch PSD $\rightarrow$ XGBoost classifier) without modifying underlying prediction logic.

\subsection{Modality 3: Speech Audio Acoustic Pipeline}
The speech acoustic engine processes $N = 591$ audio recordings (resampled/balanced to 788 instances: Cookie Theft Dementia vs. Semantic/Phonemic Fluency controls).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Biomarker Extraction}: Librosa extracts 80+ parameters including 20 MFCCs ($\Delta, \Delta^2$), fundamental frequency ($F_0$), Jitter, and Shimmer:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Jitter(local)} = \frac{\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1} |T_i - T_{i+1}|}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N T_i}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Classification}: Evaluated via a Random Forest ensemble of 200 decision trees.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multimodal Ensemble Fusion Engine}
To integrate independent modality probability outputs $P_i \in [0, 1]$ for $i \in \{\text{cognitive}, \text{eeg}, \text{speech}\}$, a constrained soft-voting ensemble is constructed. The final predicted probability $P_{\text{ensemble}}$ is expressed as:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{ensemble}} = \sum_{i \in \{\text{cog,eeg,speech}\}} W_i \cdot P_i
\end{equation}
subject to the constraints:
\begin{equation}
\sum_i W_i = 1, \quad W_i \ge 0
\end{equation}

Weights $W_i$ are optimized via Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP):
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{cognitive}} = 0.34, \quad W_{\text{eeg}} = 0.33, \quad W_{\text{speech}} = 0.33
\end{equation}

\textbf{Clinical Risk Stratification}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $P_{\text{ensemble}} < 0.35 \rightarrow$ \textbf{Low Risk (Healthy Control)}
    \item $0.35 \le P_{\text{ensemble}} \le 0.65 \rightarrow$ \textbf{Moderate Risk (MCI)}
    \item $P_{\text{ensemble}} > 0.65 \rightarrow$ \textbf{High Risk (Alzheimer's Disease)}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Explainable AI (SHAP) Layer}
SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) are computed to quantify feature contributions:
\begin{equation}
\phi_j = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} \left[f(S \cup \{j\}) - f(S)\right]
\end{equation}

\section{Implementation}
NeuroCare is implemented as a modular Python-Flask framework. The system incorporates MNE-Python for 19-channel EEG filtering, Librosa for voice acoustic processing, Scikit-Learn/XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost for model training, and SHAP for explainable AI plot generation. A responsive web portal allows clinicians to enter patient metrics, upload `.csv`/`.edf` EEG files, upload scanned PDF/image reports, or record live voice samples directly in the browser.

\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}
NeuroCare demonstrates that combining cognitive testing, 19-channel EEG spectral slowing, and voice disfluency biomarkers produces a highly accurate (96.85\%) non-invasive diagnostic platform. Future work will focus on integrating consumer 4-channel wearable EEG bands and multi-language acoustic models.
